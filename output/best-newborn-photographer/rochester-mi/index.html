Posed vs. lifestyle newborn photography

Posed newborn photography involves carefully positioning sleeping babies in wraps, props, and setups — this style requires extensive training and is typically done in the first 5–14 days when babies are sleepiest and most malleable. Lifestyle newborn photography captures your family naturally at home in the first weeks, with less posing and more documentary storytelling. Both are beautiful — choose based on which images resonate with you when you look at portfolios.

Safety is non-negotiable

Any pose that involves a baby's head being supported only by their own hands (sometimes called the 'froggy pose') should only be attempted by photographers with specific safety training, and even then it's created as a composite image. Ask any photographer you're considering about their safety training and what measures they take to protect your baby. A good photographer will welcome this question.

When to book and what to expect

Book your newborn photographer before your due date — ideally in your second trimester. Most photographers will put you on a tentative schedule and confirm once baby arrives. Sessions typically last 2–4 hours for posed work (much of that time is feeding, soothing, and waiting for baby to settle). Have the house warm, yourself fed, and expectations flexible.
