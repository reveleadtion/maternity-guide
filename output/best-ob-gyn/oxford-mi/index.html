What to look for in an OB-GYN

The most important factors: board certification, hospital affiliation (which hospital will you deliver at?), group vs. solo practice (will you always see the same provider?), their philosophy on interventions, and how accessible they are for questions between appointments. Insurance network is also a practical starting point — call your insurer for an in-network list before you start researching.

Questions to ask at your first appointment

How many providers are in the practice and who covers deliveries? What is their cesarean rate? What is their philosophy on pain management during labor? How do they handle after-hours calls and concerns? Do they support birth plans? These questions will tell you a great deal about whether their approach aligns with yours.

When to switch providers

It is completely acceptable — and sometimes necessary — to change OB-GYNs during pregnancy. If you feel dismissed, rushed, or consistently uncomfortable, trust that instinct. The earlier in your pregnancy you make a switch, the easier the transition. Most practices will request records from your previous provider on your behalf.
